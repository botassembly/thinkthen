score places a piece of text on a scale of levels you name, from lowest to highest. It prints a number from 0, the lowest level, to 1, the highest.

<Example id="score-low" />

Close to 0: a refund request is routine, not urgent.

## A higher score

<Example id="score-high" />

Higher: a double charge is more urgent than a plain refund request, but still nearer routine than urgent.

## See why

<details class="example-disclosure">
<summary>See why</summary>

`--json` prints the details. This `jq` keeps the chance of each level, lowest first.

<Example id="score-why" />

routine has 0.95 and urgent 0.05. The score weighs each level by its place on the scale, so with two levels it is the chance of the higher one. [JSON output](/reference/json-output/) lists every field.

</details>

## Use more levels

<details class="example-disclosure">
<summary>Use more levels</summary>

Name the levels with `--level`, lowest first. With three levels, the middle one counts as 0.5.

<Example id="score-levels" />

score always prints a number from 0 to 1, however many levels you name.

</details>
