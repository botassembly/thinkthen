# Resource observations

Read these limits before installing or making paid calls. The resource observations below come from the pre-release qualification campaign. They do not prove that any surface is free of leaks. Later observations cover only their measured intervals. The earlier PostgreSQL System One descriptor failure remains a historical failure.

The extended Java and TypeScript observations used Linux x86_64 with glibc 2.28 and a local backend stub, without forced collection. Each completed 60 rounds of 100,000 records. Their figures measure the change in RSS across owned processes from the completed-round 41 checkpoint to the round 60 checkpoint, over 1,900,000 subsequent verified records. Both intervals met the extended-soak floor of less than 1 byte per verified record and are recorded as finite-window warm-up or allocator retention. Their storage owners and behavior after round 60 remain unproven.

The earlier qualification campaign covered 34 surface/backend pairs. Eight PASS observations cover Ruby, C#, SQLite and PostgreSQL on both the System One and OpenAI client paths. Seven new installed-package observations and one saved historical PostgreSQL/OpenAI observation meet the extended-soak memory criterion and all other measured resource criteria. Each completed 60 rounds of 100,000 correct records against local backend stubs, with 187,500 requests and zero failed rows. Their RSS figures compare completed checkpoints 41 to 60 over 1,900,000 subsequent verified records. Three separate retention observations cover TypeScript and Java on the OpenAI client path and R on the System One client path. These three windows met the extended-soak floor and are accepted as finite-window warm-up or allocator retention under the qualification rule. Their storage owners remain unproven. These observations do not replace earlier results or establish behavior on other platforms. This is not an all-pass qualification of the new release. Use bounded workloads and monitor process resources for long-running applications.
