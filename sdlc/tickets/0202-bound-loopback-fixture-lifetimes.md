# 0202: Bound loopback fixture lifetimes

Status: landing review candidate on 2026-09-27. The accepted design received a fresh read-only Sol Medium review at `1046d8ca`; the runtime received fresh independent ACCEPT at `589a2f8c`. ## Outcome and authority

Each loopback fixture owns its listening socket, accept thread, and active connection workers for a finite lifetime. A completed case releases those resources before the next case accumulates them. While a fixture still has an owner, a scripted listener keeps its port after its planned replies and answers every extra complete request with the existing drift status. A late client of one case cannot consume another case's reply.

The authority is `sdlc/issues/2026-09-27-loopback-listeners-exhaust-the-test-process-file-limit.md`. A full backend test process under a 1,024-file soft limit passed 297 of 399 cases and then failed 102 with `Too many open files`. A retry under 4,096 passed and sampled 1,275 descriptors. The listener at `conformance/backend/src/listener.rs` moves sockets into detached accept loops and documents process-exit lifetime. Ticket 0127 made that lifetime deliberate to protect late-connection isolation; this ticket keeps the isolation while ending the ownership when client work ends. This serves the ideal state's offline shared-case gate and strict Rust checks. Ian can overturn the fixture retirement contract; it changes test support, not product behavior or the settled specification.

## Retained behavior and ownership boundary

- Bind before returning the fixture. Keep a scripted port listening after its script runs out. Skip incomplete, empty, and stalled requests without spending a scripted reply. Count connections, record complete requests, and answer an extra complete request with HTTP 500 and `the script has no reply left for this connection`. Preserve the reset drift diagnostic and answering listeners' keep-alive, events, request counts, peak count, delay, and held-reply behavior.
- A fixture owner remains alive until every child process or client thread that could use its copied address has exited or been joined. A copied URL is not an ownership token. The final owner retires the fixture synchronously. Only then may the port be released and reused. This is the finite safety boundary: no test may send a late request after its final owner has retired.
- Use ordinary shared ownership, such as `Arc<Listener>`, when a caller must drop one handle before its last possible late connection. The existing after-drop regression must hold another owner, prove that the late connection still receives the 500 drift response, then release the final owner and prove retirement. Do not retain a global list of old ports or add a timer that guesses when a late client is done.
- Audit callers that copy `base`, `origin`, or `url` into an asynchronous child or thread. Keep the fixture owner through the child's wait or thread join. The secrecy sweep already waits for each command before its local listener leaves scope; retain that ordering and its connection/request failure diagnostic. The conformance backend binary must cancel and join pending `wait` tasks at input close before its listener retires.

## Change and lifetime design

1. Give `Listener` a shutdown state and ownership of its accept thread. Both scripted and answering accept loops stop on retirement. Do not use an accept loop that waits forever in blocking `incoming()`. Stop acceptance, wake or close the listener, join the accept thread, and close the listening socket before final retirement returns. Synchronize the stop check with connection registration so a connection cannot spawn an untracked worker during shutdown.
2. Track every accepted connection and its worker. Retirement closes active sockets to interrupt idle reads and peeks, wakes interruptible delays and held replies, and joins workers after the accept thread can spawn no more. Take join handles out of locks before joining. The scripted path may stay sequential, but its active read and held reply must obey the same stop. Preserve request and response ordering while the owner lives. An active request deliberately abandoned by final retirement need not receive a response; the caller contract requires its client to finish first on the successful path.
3. Replace fixture-held `std::sync::Barrier` waits with a cancellation-aware rendezvous for `Canned::after_release`. Keep the existing arrival/release ordering, including the two-worker rendezvous in recognize and the multi-worker interrupt test. Retirement wakes participants so a panic or early return cannot leave a worker holding a socket or make `Drop` wait forever. Make the `held_by` gate and response delay retirement-aware too. This is a harness API change only; do not add a product export, flag, or hook. Update the seven current Rust callers of `after_release` named in `opens`.
4. Keep retirement idempotent and independent of response-script exhaustion. The last owner ends both scripted and answering fixtures, including a fixture that received zero requests. Do not raise the gate's file limit as the fix. In `conformance/backend/src/arms.rs`, retain asynchronous `wait N` while input is open, but give each task the run's cancellation signal and track its join handle. On input close, cancel and join pending waits before retiring the listener, then print the final count once. Direct `Backend::wait` callers keep the five-second bound. Preserve `conformance/backend/tests/binary.rs`'s existing proof that input close with a pending `wait 1` exits in under one second and prints only the final count.

The fixture keeps the ownership mechanics in `conformance/backend/src/lifetime.rs`, declared as an internal module by `conformance/backend/src/lib.rs`. The existing listener file has 492 nonblank lines against the 500-line file ceiling before this change. The new module owns the shutdown state, tracked sockets and workers, and cancellable rendezvous used by the listener. It adds no product export or hook. This file split changes none of the retirement or ordering contracts above. Its exact files must be claimed on main before implementation.

The builder may simplify the worker and gate mechanics after a red boundary proof, but the final design must be synchronous, cancellation-safe, and finite. If a callback can block outside the fixture's cancellation control, show that all such callers finish before retirement or revise the callback boundary before claiming complete worker ownership. A detached thread, grace timer, or best-effort join does not meet this ticket.
