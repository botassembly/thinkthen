# 0365: A forked child on macOS answers on every surface that waits on the calling thread

Status: ready. Plan: `sdlc/planning/cleanup-2026-09-30.md`, lane claude-4. Issue: `sdlc/issues/2026-10-01-macos-forked-children-crash-on-a-channel-wait.md`.

## Outcome

A host that asks once, forks, and asks again in the child gets an answer in the child on macOS. This holds for the Rust API, the C door and every host over it, Python (calls and streams), R, SQLite and DuckDB. Every wait that can block a host's calling thread goes through one channel the crate owns, `thinkthen::fork_safe`, or through a thread join. That channel waits on a `Mutex` and `Condvar`. On macOS those are pthread calls that survive `fork()`, while Rust's channel waits and `thread::scope` park the thread on a libdispatch semaphore that a forked child cannot use. Linux behaviour is unchanged.

## Evidence

- Starts from: Quick Fix `65e2fcb5a` proved the cause on the DuckDB bridge and replaced its one wait with a local mutex and condition variable (`Answer` and `Giver` in `databases/duckdb/bridge/src/ffi.rs`). On 2026-10-01 a fork probe on the M5 (macOS 26.4, arm64, Rust 1.95.0) ran each surface's existing or minimal fork case 10 times on main `f056e8cfd`. In every case the parent asks, forks, and the child asks again.

  | Surface | Case | Children that died | Where the child crashed |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Rust API | `fork-probe` `a_warm_parent_engine_and_its_clone_answer_in_the_child`, `a_child_replays_the_parents_warm_cache_without_sending`, `a_default_engine_from_the_parent_answers_in_the_child` | 10 of 10 runs, SIGTRAP | `std::sync::mpsc::Receiver::recv_timeout` in `engine::pipeline::run::Run::receive`, under `workers::scoped` and `Engine::ask_all` |
  | C door | `libraries/c/tests/c/fork.c` | 10 of 10, wait status 5 (SIGTRAP) | the same engine path; the door calls the Rust API on the caller's thread |
  | Python | `decide`, fork, `decide` in the child, as `test_a_forked_child_answers_and_the_parent_counts_nothing` does | 9 of 10; the tenth failed in the parent on Debt 020's closed connection | `recv_timeout` in `libraries/python/src/worker.rs` `wait` |
  | SQLite | `thinkthen_decide`, fork, `thinkthen_decide` in the child on the inherited connection | 10 of 10, SIGTRAP | the calling-thread `recv_timeout` in `databases/sqlite/src/worker.rs` `wait` |
  | R | `libraries/r/tests/fork.R` (`parallel::mcparallel`) | 10 of 10 | `recv_timeout` in `calls/worker.rs` `wait`, inlined into `calls::decide` |

  Every crash report reads "BUG IN CLIENT OF LIBDISPATCH: Use-after-free of dispatch_semaphore_t" and "crashed on child side of fork pre-exec". Rust 1.93 and 1.95 sources confirm that on Apple targets `Mutex` and `Condvar` use pthread, the thread parker uses libdispatch, and `thread::scope` parks the calling thread until its running count reaches zero. A scope whose threads were all joined first does not park.
- Not ticketed, with reasons:
  - PostgreSQL waits through `std::sync::mpsc` in `databases/postgresql/src/call.rs`, but no backend can reach the crash. The postmaster runs no call (`_PG_init` only registers settings; see `databases/postgresql/src/lib.rs` at `_PG_init`), and a backend never forks after a call. The crash needs the forking thread to have blocked once before the fork.
  - Ruby's calling thread already waits on its own `Mutex` and `Condvar` (`libraries/ruby/src/lib.rs`), and the engine runs on a thread the child starts fresh.
  - TypeScript and the hosts over the C door (Go, C#, the JVM languages, Swift, PHP, Zig, Dart, C++, Ada, Objective-C, COBOL) have no calling-thread wait of their own. They reach the engine through the C door or the Rust API, so the engine change covers them.
  - The command: its process is never forked by a host after a call, and its own schedulers in `cli/schedule` keep their channels.
  - `public/options.rs` keeps its bounded attempt channel. Engine workers send into it, and the calling thread drains it without blocking.
- Keeps: every call's answers, counts, request totals, cancel, deadline and interrupt behaviour; the tick lengths (`Cancel::poll`, 50 ms binding ticks, the pipeline pause); the order in which a binding reads its token, its stop and an answer that is already waiting (issue `closed/2026-09-27-a-binding-wait-loop-lets-an-answer-beat-a-stop-in-the-same-tick.md`); the "ended with no answer" defects when a worker drops its sender unsent; the DuckDB bridge's thread name, cancel token and detach on a stopped query; panic containment on every worker; the existing fork regressions of ticket 0096 and the quick fix. The PostgreSQL extension, Ruby, TypeScript and the command are not edited.
- Changes:
  - New `crates/thinkthen/src/engine/fork_safe.rs`, about 90 lines: `channel()`, a cloneable `Sender<T>` whose `send` never blocks, and a `Receiver<T>` with `recv` and `recv_timeout`. It returns std's own `SendError`, `RecvError` and `RecvTimeoutError`, re-exported, so call sites keep their matches. State is one `Mutex` holding a queue, the live sender count and whether the receiver lives, plus one `Condvar`. A last sender's drop wakes the receiver, which then reads `Disconnected` once the queue is empty. A dropped receiver makes later sends fail and drops what was queued, as std does. The `Receiver` is `Sync`, so scoped workers share one by reference and Python waits on it inside `py.detach` without moving it in and out. The module's comment names the macOS cause and this ticket. `core` is untouched. The public API re-exports the module as `#[doc(hidden)] pub use crate::engine::fork_safe;` in `public/mod.rs`, because the bindings reach the crate only through its public API. It is a tool for ThinkThen's own bindings, not a documented interface.
  - `engine/workers.rs`: `on_worker` and `scoped_observed` use the channel. `scoped_observed` keeps each worker's handle and joins every one before its scope closes, also while the body unwinds, so the scope never parks the calling thread. A worker's panic still reaches the caller. The work queue becomes unbounded: the pipeline sends only while fewer than `jobs` requests are out, so the old bound of `jobs` never blocked.
  - `engine/pipeline.rs` and `engine/pipeline/run.rs`: the `ask_all` events channel, its `Port` and the worker queue use the channel. The command's `reader` asks channel is unchanged.
  - `public/pull.rs`: the calling thread's events channel uses it.
  - `libraries/python/src/worker.rs` and `src/stream.rs`: the call wait, the stream's events and its input use it. The stream's input was a rendezvous (`sync_channel(0)`); the worker asks for each record with `Need` and waits for it, so a buffered send carries the same records in the same order. `libraries/python/NOTES.md` drops the sentence about moving the receiver.
  - `libraries/r/thinkthen/src/rust/src/calls/worker.rs`, `databases/sqlite/src/worker.rs`: the call wait uses it.
  - `databases/duckdb/bridge/src/ffi.rs`: `run_detached` uses the channel. `Answer` and `Giver` go: a worker that ends without sending drops its sender, and the caller reads the same "ended with no answer" defect.
  - Ratchets move to their measured totals: `sdlc/ratchet.json` and each changed binding's `ratchet.json`.
- Proof:
  - New regression `conformance/consumer/fork-probe/tests/channel.rs`, a table of the channel's blocking waits across a real fork. The parent's test thread first blocks on the channel, so a std channel would have parked it. Then in the child, for each row a worker thread sleeps 30 ms so the wait truly blocks: `recv` gets a value, `recv_timeout` gets a value, `recv` reads the end when the worker drops its sender unsent, and `recv_timeout` times out with the sender alive. It runs in the Linux test rung with the rest of `fork-probe`. On the M5 the same table with `std::sync::mpsc` put in for the channel must crash its child; the build record shows that run.
  - The surface regressions already exist and run on Linux in their checks: the `fork-probe` engine cases, the C door's `fork.c` row, Python's `test_a_forked_child_answers_and_the_parent_counts_nothing`, R's `tests/fork.R`, SQLite's `test_a_forked_child_hears_its_own_interrupt` and `test_a_grandchild_hears_its_own_interrupt`, and DuckDB's `a_forked_child_answers_from_a_zero_total`. Each failed on the M5 at `f056e8cfd` as the table above shows, or in the quick fix for DuckDB.
  - On Linux, one at a time while the one-minute load stays at or under 16: `CARGO_NET_OFFLINE=true python3 sdlc/scripts/policy.py`, `sdlc/scripts/tickets`, workspace clippy with `-D warnings` on all targets, `sdlc/scripts/test` (which runs the crate tests and `fork-probe`), `lint` in a clean checkout, the C door check, and the surface checks of the changed bindings: `libraries/python/check.sh`, `libraries/r/check.sh`, `databases/sqlite/check.sh` and `databases/duckdb/check.sh`.
  - On the M5, at the branch head: 10 runs each of the probe table above (Rust `fork-probe`, `fork.c`, Python, SQLite, R) with no child crash; the new channel table with no crash, and with std's channel put in, a crash; 10 runs of the DuckDB fork case. A parent failure from Debt 020's connection race is reported as such, not as a pass.
- Defers: the PostgreSQL wait, until a host model can fork a process after a call. A policy check that keeps `std::sync::mpsc` out of calling-thread code; the fork regressions guard the behaviour, and the command and the attempt channel would need exceptions. `Once` and `OnceLock` also park on macOS, but only when two threads race the first initialization, which a single-threaded child does not do. Waits on worker threads, since a child starts those fresh.
